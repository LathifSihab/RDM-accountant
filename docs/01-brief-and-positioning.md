## Stated values

Supplied by the client, in their order:

1. Sustainability
2. Paperless
3. 24/7 personal contact availability
4. Client oriented

These are not four separate selling points. Treated as four, they produce the standard accountancy-website value grid that says nothing. Treated as a single argument, they say something specific: *RDM runs without paper, which is why it can run without an office visit, which is why your accountant is reachable when you need them.* Sustainability is the reason, paperless is the method, availability is the result, client-oriented is the posture. The site should be built on that chain, not on a four-tile grid.

## The reference sites, and what is actually usable

**astro.tax** — the structural model the client asked for. Dutch, playful, mascot-led, video hero, one fixed monthly price, and a "calculate your monthly rate" call to action repeated on every screen. Their strongest move is removing price anxiety before the first conversation. Their weakest point, from RDM's perspective, is that at over a thousand clients personal contact is a promise operated at scale, and their published commitment is a 24-hour response on working days.

**konsilanto.be** — a full-service Flemish firm with several offices. Video hero, a service-tile grid, named core values, and an active news feed. Warmer and more senior in register than astro. Useful for tone.

**rsm.global/belgium/nl** — a global network firm. Mega-menu, insight-heavy, trilingual, formal address, sustainability as a named service line. Useful as a credibility reference and as a warning: RDM cannot carry this much structure, and copying its formality would bury the thing that makes RDM worth choosing.

## Positioning

> *Proposal — awaiting client sign-off. See notes.md §3.*

RDM is a one-office CommV. Both reference poles are unwinnable head-on: it cannot out-scale RSM on network credibility, and it cannot out-spend astro.tax on platform engineering and client volume. The opportunity is the gap between them.

An online-only firm gives you convenience and takes away the person. A network firm gives you the person and takes away the speed. RDM's claim is that you don't have to choose: the paperless workflow is what frees up the accountant to actually be available.

**Positioning statement (internal, not for publication):**
For Antwerp entrepreneurs who want their bookkeeping to be effortless without becoming anonymous, RDM Accountants is a certified practice that runs entirely without paper — so that the person handling your file has the time to answer you personally.

**Public-facing proposition (nl-BE):**
*Papierloos werken, zodat er tijd overblijft voor jou.*

This line does the work of all four values at once and is defensible for a small firm in a way that "the biggest" or "the most advanced" would not be.

## Audience

Primary: Antwerp-based freelancers and owner-managed SMEs up to roughly 20 staff. Consultants, creative and medical professionals, tradespeople, small retail and hospitality. Includes starters, who are high-value because they have no incumbent accountant to leave.

Secondary: entrepreneurs already with an accountant who feel unheard — slow replies, surprise invoices, annual accounts explained once and never discussed. The switching journey matters to this group and is given its own section on the Services page.

Not the audience: large corporates, listed companies, statutory audit clients. RSM's territory. The site should not pretend otherwise; pretending costs credibility with the actual audience.

## Tone of voice

Dutch (nl-BE), informal **"je"** throughout. Warm and plainspoken in the manner of konsilanto, structurally clear in the manner of astro, without astro's mascot register.

The mascot question is settled by the logo. The existing mark is a high-contrast Didone monogram — editorial, closer to a fashion masthead than to an accountancy logo. A cartoon character fights it. The eye-catching quality the client asked for has to come from typography, the green, and one or two orchestrated moments of motion, not from illustration.

Rules for writers:

- Say the thing, then stop. No "in today's fast-moving business environment".
- Numbers over adjectives. "Antwoord dezelfde werkdag" beats "razendsnelle service".
- No jargon without translation. If "UBO-registratie" appears, one clause explains it.
- Never name a competitor.
- Sentence case in headings. No tracked-out capitals.
- Every call to action says what happens next, in the same words as the page it leads to.

## What makes this site an eyecatcher

The client asked for an eyecatcher. Spending that budget in one place rather than five:

**The memorable element is the cashflow quote, treated as a full-bleed typographic moment** — deep forest green, the line set large in Playfair italic, with a single slow horizontal drift on entry. Everything around it stays disciplined. This is the screenshot people take.

Secondary, quieter distinctions:

- A paper counter on the homepage showing sheets of paper not printed since founding — turns the sustainability claim into a number instead of an adjective.
- The simulator's result appears as a typeset figure in the display serif rather than as a pricing card, so the price feels like an answer rather than a plan tier.
- Editorial asymmetry in the layout, inherited from the logo's off-centre M.

## Success criteria

The demo is working if:

1. A visitor can reach an indicative monthly price within roughly two minutes of landing.
2. The four values are legible without being listed as four tiles.
3. It is obvious within one screen that RDM is in Antwerp and certified.
4. It does not look like astro.tax, konsilanto.be, or RSM — it uses what they do well without wearing their clothes.
